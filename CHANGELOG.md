v3.0.38 - 2026-08-14 - Joe's screenshot after v3.0.37 shipped: X cutaway showed correct red poché, but Y and the height cut (Z) showed the plain translucent shell instead -- no poché at all. Root cause, confirmed conclusively live (not theorized): three.js splits its render list into two hard-separated passes -- ALL opaque objects render first, THEN all transparent objects, as two entirely separate batches. renderOrder only sorts objects WITHIN each of those two lists; it has no effect on ordering ACROSS them (checked directly against three.js's own render-list source, not assumed from behavior). The 6 stencil-write meshes used for each axis's parity test are colorWrite:false but otherwise plain opaque materials (transparent was never set), while the 3 poché quads are transparent:true (needed for their 0.4-opacity look since v3.0.29). That meant ALL SIX stencil-parity writes (X, Y, AND Z) always completed first, bundled into one opaque batch -- only then did the 3 quads render, in sequence. X's quad (rendering first among the three, at its own renderOrder 1.1) read the just-written parity correctly, but its onAfterRender handler calls renderer.clearStencil(), which wipes WebGL's ENTIRE shared 8-bit stencil buffer -- there's no way to clear just one axis's share of it. By the time Y's quad rendered next, it was reading an all-zero buffer, and NotEqualStencilFunc(ref=0) can never pass against an all-zero buffer, so Y drew nothing. Z, rendering last, was cleared a second time over by the same mechanism before it ever got a turn.
